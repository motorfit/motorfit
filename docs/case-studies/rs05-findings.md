# RS05 findings

- Formal parameters describe one RS05 (UID 542b38001c443916), its measured fixture and the recorded PD trajectories. RS01/02/06 protocol profiles are present; no formal parameter identification is claimed for them. RS01/RS06 historical checks had narrower scope.
- M1 is an effective mechanical model. `torque_ratio` is not an independent electrical Kt/R measurement; the CAN torque field is firmware nominal torque, not a shaft torque sensor.
- M1–M6 were selected with the declared training/validation split before opening the final test. Later mechanism investigations are diagnostic studies, not a new held-out selection.
- CPU/GPU agreement with the full NoSlip configuration is established for the recorded benchmark and versions. An older MJWarp path lacked NoSlip; that old limitation is not a claim about the verified 3.17 path. Robot contact dynamics remain untested.
- A representative 200 g release has about 2.714 mrad of measured retreat on the specified processed metric. Frozen M1 predicts no retreat and a stop error near 7.116 mrad. Aligning to raw STOP send reduces that stop error only to about 7.068 mrad. Timing improves the early release trajectory but does not explain the missing retreat.
- Dahl/LuGre, elastic and sensor/filter candidate studies gave some tracking improvements, but did not jointly explain recoil and stop behavior across loads. Their parameters were not promoted to the formal M1. Summary tables/figures remain in `data/rs05/results/comparisons`; detailed candidate runs remain in the external research archive.
- The 500 g peak-to-stop displacement includes a large backswing; it is not the same regime as the small 200 g retreat. Metric windows/filtering and raw versus resampled signals must be kept distinct when comparing reports.
- A received feedback timestamp is not an internal sensor sample time. STOP send and reply timestamps are not direct measurements of power-stage shutdown. Static final Iq=0 does not establish the entire in-motion current history.
